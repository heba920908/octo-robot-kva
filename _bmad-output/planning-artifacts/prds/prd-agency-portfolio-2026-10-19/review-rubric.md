# PRD Quality Review — kVA Instalaciones Landing Page (second pass, after the 2026-10-21 Update)

## Overall verdict
The Update held together well. The four brochure-based Services, real Projects, Nosotros (FR-18), Clientes (FR-19), Direct contact links (FR-20), the single published channel, and the interim Legal identity all show up consistently in the Glossary, FRs, NFR-5, §6, §7, §8, the SMs, and the addendum. All four high findings from the prior review are still fixed. The PRD is ready for `bmad-architecture`. The remaining risk is again at **launch**. The Update created new launch dependencies, such as brochure photo licenses, the format of the published phone number, and kVA's approval of copy, but §8.3 does not list them. Two edge-case gaps also came with the new Clientes list: FR-10 and FR-19 contradict each other when no Trust item is confirmed, and the Client entity is ambiguous. Fix the high finding and the FR-10/FR-19 conflict before `bmad-create-epics-and-stories`.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
The decisions from the Update are written as decisions, and each one names what was given up. The addendum's new "Second contact channel: options considered" section rejects the UX proposal to "show both pairs", and the PRD records the outcome in §7 ("Reason: user decision (2026-10-21)"). "Legal identity: options considered" names the rejected path ("Block launch…") and states the cost of the chosen path: "the notice is LFPDPPP-incomplete until then". §10 Q10 openly admits a weakness that the brochure seed brings to UJ-2: "7 of 9 under Instalaciones, so that filter option barely narrows". That is honest, decision-ready writing.

The weak point is blocking status. Q1–Q6 each say whether they block development or launch (for example, Q2 "Not launch-blocking", Q6 "Not launch-blocking (user decision…)"). Q7–Q11 say nothing about it, and three of them gate content that §8.1 puts in scope at launch. This is covered under Scope honesty.

## Substance over theater — strong
There is still no persona, vision, or NFR theater. The brochure material is filtered through the thesis rather than pasted in. §2.4 bans "los mejores", "líderes", "los más altos estándares", and "superar expectativas" "including where brochure copy is reused". FR-18 makes that ban testable for Nosotros ("contains none of the unsupported superlatives listed in §2.4" and "'kVA' (not the brochure's 'KVA')"). The §2.4 Per-Service hooks connect each brochure Service to a concrete buyer risk (GDMTH, power factor below 90%, ANSI/TIA-568, UVIE). That is earned domain substance.

### Findings
- **low** SM-3 is an acceptance test presented as a success metric (§9 SM-3, "verified once before launch") — it duplicates FR-5 and FR-15 consequences and validates nothing after launch. *Fix:* Move it to §8.3 as a launch check, or recast it as an operational metric (for example, "a content change ships in ≤ 1 working day").

## Strategic coherence — adequate
The thesis is still clear and still drives the features: "*these people do serious electrical work, to standard*", backed by evidence, with contact one step away. The Update strengthens it. Real Projects replace the old placeholder-only launch, the Clientes list adds third-party proof, and FR-20 gives UJ-1's edge case a real phone fallback. The Lead definition (§3) and SM-1 now count calls and untagged emails, and they state the undercount ("expected to undercount"). This closes the prior SM-1 blind spot. SM-C1 and SM-C2 remain real counterweights.

The thesis is weakest where the Update added self-description rather than evidence, and where a claim can bypass the evidence gate.

### Findings
- **medium** Nosotros (FR-18) says it realizes journeys that never use it (§4.5 "Realizes UJ-2, UJ-3"; FR-18 "Realizes UJ-2") — neither Jorge (UJ-2: Projects, then "the **Trust section** for years in business, standards, and the **Clientes list**") nor Ana (UJ-3: Hero, Services, Contact) reads Nosotros. A section of mission, vision, and values is the least evidence-based content on a page whose positioning "holds only if the page shows evidence" (§2.4). Including it is a recorded decision. The traceability claim, however, is nominal, and story creation will take it at face value. *Fix:* Add a real step to UJ-2 (for example, Jorge checks the values for *Seguridad* before a shortlist review), or state plainly that FR-18 is brand and brochure parity with no journey dependency, so epics rank it below Trust and Projects.
- **low** The Hero proof point bypasses the Trust confirmation gate (FR-4, "at least one proof point (for example, years in business or nationwide coverage)"; §4.6, "Only evidence-backed Trust items are shown") — the same claims that FR-10 holds back until "confirmed" can appear on the Hero from the hero doc with no confirmation. EXPERIENCE.md makes this explicit: the proof point is "independent of Trust items". *Fix:* Require the Hero proof point to repeat a confirmed Trust item, or give it the same confirmed flag.
- **low** The Lead definition and SM-1 disagree on WhatsApp (§3 Lead, "Once the **WhatsApp CTA** is enabled, chats started from the page count too"; §9 SM-1 counts only tagged emails, calls, and untagged emails) — *Fix:* Add "and, once enabled, WhatsApp chats that start with the Lead subject tag" to SM-1.
- **low** The content accepted risk leaves out the Clientes list (§8.3, "Accepted risk (content)") — UJ-2 relies on the Clientes list, but every Client needs "written permission" (Q2), which is not launch-blocking. Launch may therefore show no Clientes list at all (FR-19, "If no Client is permitted, the Clientes list is not rendered"). This state is allowed but never named as an accepted risk. *Fix:* Add one clause to the content accepted risk.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
The new FRs are as testable as the old ones. FR-20 is a model: it specifies the `tel:` international format, the subject "Lead subject tag followed by 'Contacto'", "work without JavaScript", a build failure on a missing address or invalid phone, and "proyectos2@… and (56) 4029 4029 appear nowhere on the page or in its metadata". FR-8's carousel consequences ("{i} de {n}", "swiping is never the only way", one-photo Projects show no controls) and FR-19's permission rule ("does not render anywhere on the page or in its metadata") also translate directly into tests. Two gaps from the Update, however, will produce conflicting or underspecified tests.

### Findings
- **medium** FR-10 and FR-19 contradict each other when Trust items are absent but Clients are permitted (§4.6) — FR-10: "If no Trust items are confirmed, the section and its header link are hidden." FR-19 covers only the opposite case: "If no Client is permitted, the Clientes list is not rendered; Trust items are unaffected." With zero confirmed Trust items and one or more permitted Clients, FR-10 requires a hidden section and FR-19 requires visible Clients. Under test-first rules, both tests cannot pass. FR-1 ("hidden because it has no content to show (FR-10, FR-18)") inherits the ambiguity. *Fix:* Change FR-10 to "If no Trust items are confirmed **and no Client is permitted**, the section and its header link are hidden. Otherwise, only the empty part is omitted." Add FR-19 to FR-1's list.
- **medium** The Client entity and its unit of permission are ambiguous (§3 Client, "an organization kVA has worked for"; §4.6, "names the organizations kVA has worked for, grouped as in kVA's brochure (for example, Oficinas Gubernamentales, Inmobiliaria Goytia, CHR Construcciones)"; FR-19 "grouped under the group named in their Content doc") — the §4.6 examples are the brochure's *groups*, not Clients. In the launch seed (EXPERIENCE.md › Clientes), the groups are often the client organizations themselves, and the items are their sites or branches ("Guadalajara, Morelia, Culiacán…", "Corporativo, Torre Axis") or end clients of a general contractor ("Keyence, The Pool, Sulzer" under CHR Construcciones). Getting written permission for a branch such as "Guadalajara" is meaningless, and for end clients reached through CHR it is unclear who grants permission. The Project "client name" proof field (FR-8) carries a second, unlinked permission (Q2, "per Client (FR-19) and per Project client field (FR-8)"), so the two can disagree. This feeds the `client` schema directly. *Fix:* Define Client as the organization that grants permission. Make the group either that organization (with its sites as sub-items) or a category, and say which. State which level carries the permission flag. Make FR-8's client field a reference to a Client doc so there is a single permission.
- **low** FR-9 does not say what happens when a photo has no source or license (FR-9, "records its source and license") — FR-8 makes a missing `alt` fail the build, and the addendum's `project` schema lists `source` and `license` as photo fields, but the FR does not say that missing values fail. Q8 makes this matter at launch (see Scope honesty). *Fix:* Add "a Project photo without `source` or `license` fails the build", and define an acceptable value for kVA-owned brochure photos.
- **low** "Confirmed" and "evidence-backed" are not the same bar (§4.6, "Only evidence-backed Trust items are shown"; FR-10 renders anything "marked as confirmed", and the evidence reference is optional) — *Fix:* Align the wording. Either the confirmed flag means kVA confirmed the claim in writing, or the evidence reference is required for registrations and certifications.
- **low** The rule that the hook Sub-service is listed first does not fit the hook list (§4.3, "The Sub-service that carries the card's §2.4 hook is listed first") — Instalaciones has two hook Sub-services (*Canalización y cableado* and *Voz/Datos*), the *Servicios de Datos* hook names no Sub-service, and the *Puesta en marcha* hook mixes tests with *Validación de calidad de energía*. *Fix:* Say "hook Sub-services are listed first", and name exactly one Sub-service per hook or mark the rule as content guidance.
- **low** The zero-Projects and single-Service filter states are undefined (FR-7; FR-1) — FR-1 lets only Trust and Nosotros hide. Nothing says what Proyectos does with zero Projects. EXPERIENCE.md hides the filter "when fewer than two Services have Projects [ASSUMPTION]", a rule FR-7 does not contain. *Fix:* Add both states to FR-7, or explicitly delegate them to the spine.
- **low** Turning on WhatsApp changes the privacy data flow without a requirement to update the notice (FR-17, "ready to switch on without code changes"; §4.8, "data reaches kVA only in the email the Visitor sends or the call the Visitor makes") — WhatsApp sends data through Meta. *Fix:* Add an FR-17 consequence or a Q1 note: the Privacy notice Content doc is updated in the same change that sets the number.

## Scope honesty — adequate
§7 Non-Goals now carries the second-channel decision with its reason. §8.2 remains honest, and §8.3 still separates development from launch and names owners and accepted risks for content and legal. The Assumptions Index round-trips (11 inline tags and 11 entries, in document order). Open-items density is 11 assumptions, 11 Open Questions, and no `[NOTE FOR PM]`. That is fine for building, because development is unblocked. It is too loose for launch, because five of the questions have no stated blocking status.

### Findings
- **high** The launch gate was not updated for the dependencies the Update introduced (§8.3; §8.1; §10 Q7, Q8, Q11; FR-9; FR-18; FR-20) — §8.3 lists only hosting/DNS, share image and logo, and SM-2. Yet the Update makes launch depend on three answers from kVA that have no stated status:
  1. **Q8 photo licenses:** §8.1 publishes "the real Projects from kVA's 2025 brochure" with photos, and FR-9 requires "source and license" for every Project photo, but Q8 asks whether those photos are licensed for web use at all.
  2. **Q11 phone format:** FR-20 publishes (56) 4000 3344 as the only phone. A wrong `tel:` breaks the page's only call channel and the JSON-LD `telephone`.
  3. **Q7 copy approval:** §4.5 says "kVA approves the wording before publication", but nothing says whether launch waits for that approval or ships without Nosotros (FR-18's absent-doc state).

  Q9 (fonts) and Q10 (Project-to-Service mapping) also have no status. For a launch-stakes site, the team cannot tell what must be true on go-live day. This is a partial regression of the prior high finding "Launch is undefined" (see Regressions). *Fix:* Give every Open Question a status of dev-blocking, launch-blocking, or non-blocking with accepted risk. Add Q8 and Q11 to §8.3, or record the fallback for each (for example, "a photo without a confirmed license is omitted; a Project with no remaining photos is not published", and "the phone is hidden until the format is confirmed"). State the Q7 fallback (for example, "launch without Nosotros").

## Downstream usability — adequate
Inside the PRD, the Update is clean. New terms (Sub-service, Project carousel, Nosotros section, Client, Clientes list, Contact channels, Direct contact link, Legal identity, Header contact CTA) are defined and used in bold. The §6 behaviour-model list (13 models) matches the addendum and EXPERIENCE.md one-to-one. The addendum's schemas cover every new content type (`client`, `nosotros`, and `site` holding only published channels). The PRD falls short in how it relates to the UX spines it now depends on.

### Findings
- **medium** The UX spines still contradict the PRD on contact channels, and the PRD has no precedence rule (§0, "Visual and interaction detail lives in those spines"; the addendum names EXPERIENCE.md › Information Architecture as the "launch seed") — EXPERIENCE.md "Overrides to the PRD" item 3 still says "contact name, both emails, and both phones as `tel:` links". Its Contact section pattern still says "Emails… each paired with its phone; a copy button per email". Its FR traceability table stops at FR-17, listing Nosotros as "Override 4" and Clientes under FR-10. Only its Open Question 9 says proyectos2@ is "Resolved". `bmad-architecture` and `bmad-create-epics-and-stories` will read both documents, and the spine contradicts FR-20 and §7 in text a story writer is likely to copy. *Fix:* Add one sentence to §0: "Where a spine conflicts with this PRD on scope, content, or published channels, the PRD wins; the spines govern visual and interaction detail." Then refresh EXPERIENCE.md Override 3, the Contact section pattern, and the traceability table to cover FR-18–FR-20.

This is still a multi-stakeholder B2B marketing page at the top of the planning chain (UX → architecture → stories). Its three UJs with named protagonists carry real weight, and grouping FRs by page section matches the build. The Update added three FRs and one section without making the document heavier: 20 FRs is proportionate for one page plus a content pipeline, and the technical detail stays in the addendum. No findings.

## Mechanical notes
- **ID order:** the §4 note correctly explains that IDs are stable and that "Adding §4.5 Nosotros moved the later subsections down by one". Within §4.7, though, the order is FR-11, FR-12, FR-13, **FR-20, FR-17**, so FR-20 comes before FR-17 even inside one section. Listing them in ascending order (FR-17, then FR-20) costs nothing. The overall document order (…FR-9, FR-18, FR-10, FR-19, FR-11–13, FR-20, FR-17, FR-14…) is acceptable given the note.
- **Section renumbering:** inside the PRD, every § reference uses the new numbers (§11's §4.6, §4.7 entries, the §8.3 and §10 Q6 references, and the addendum's "PRD §7", "§8.3, §10 Q6", "§10 Q11"). Outside it, `ux-designs/…/reconcile-prd.md` (for example, row 14, "§4.5 Trust") and EXPERIENCE.md Open Question 1 use the pre-update numbers. EXPERIENCE.md labels this; reconcile-prd.md does not. Downstream workflows should cite FR IDs, not § numbers.
- **Glossary drift:**
  - "Trust section" (UJ-2 in bold, §3 Clientes list) and "Contact section" (UJ-3, §4.7, FR-12, FR-20) are used as terms but not defined. Header anchors call them "Confianza" and "Contacto".
  - "Proof point" (FR-4, Hero) and "proof fields" (FR-8, Project) are different concepts with near-identical names.
  - "Client Content doc entry" (FR-19) suggests one doc with entries. §3 ("defined in a Content doc") and the addendum (`content/clients/*.md`) suggest one doc per Client.
  - The §3 **Lead subject tag** is defined as a prefix on "every email generated by the page", but FR-17 also applies it to the WhatsApp message.
  - FR-1 refers to "the mobile breakpoint (a design token)", while §3 and §4.7 hard-code "768 CSS px" for the composer. EXPERIENCE.md uses 1024 for the menu and 768 for the composer, so these are two breakpoints. Name both as tokens.
- **Behaviour-model mapping:** §6 places the "Cotiza un proyecto similar" entry point under the Contact composer model, but EXPERIENCE.md › Foundation maps it to `service-cta-prefill`. Pick one so the story and its acceptance IDs land in one model.
- **NFR-4 note for architecture:** JSON-LD is a data block and is not executed under `script-src`, so "allowed by hash" is harmless but unnecessary. Angular SSG critical-CSS inlining (a media-swap `onload`) can conflict with a strict `default-src 'self'`. The ADR should check this.
- **UJ-1 wording:** "the page shows the address with a one-tap copy button instead" implies the page detects a missing email client. FR-12 and the addendum ("Neither the browser nor the site can detect whether a mail client opened") show it after every submit. Say "also shows".
- **Assumptions Index:** 11 inline tags and 11 entries, matched one-to-one and in document order.
- **Cross-references:** the brief, addendum, DESIGN.md, and EXPERIENCE.md links resolve, as do `assets/media/icon.png`, `assets/media/brochure/projects/`, the UX `imports/brochure-text.md`, and the `angular.json` budgets (1 MB / 8 kB). The constitution v1.0.0 references (II behaviour models, III test-first, IV schemas/tokens, UI library decided by ADR) match `.specify/memory/constitution.md`.
- **Required sections:** all sections expected for this stakes level and product type are present.
