# PRD Quality Review — kVA Instalaciones Landing Page

## Overall verdict
This is a tight, well-formed PRD. The thesis is specific to kVA, the glossary is unusually disciplined, the three UJs each drive concrete FRs, and the addendum maps directly onto the constitution's behaviour models and schemas. `bmad-ux` and `bmad-architecture` can start from it. The risk is at **launch, not build**. The PRD allows the site to ship with no proof behind its "serious work, to standard" thesis. It never defines what "launched" means or who owns it. It omits the legal inputs that the Privacy notice needs. It also contains one FR contradiction (FR-5 vs FR-7) that will break test-first story work. Fix the four high findings before `bmad-create-epics-and-stories`.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
Decisions are stated as decisions rather than considerations: mailto-only contact (§4.6), no free-text field (§8.2, "deferred by decision"), hidden-not-disabled WhatsApp (FR-17), and generic Privacy notice (§4.7). The addendum's "Contact mechanism: options considered" names what was given up with each option. For example, under option A, "no email client on the device means no Lead," which is why FR-12 is mandatory. Open Questions §10 are actually open, and each has an owner.

The PRD avoids two tensions that a client-side reader would raise. First, the thesis depends on evidence that may not exist at launch (see Strategic coherence). Second, the legal inputs for the Privacy notice are treated as kVA's later "legal review" (§10 Q3, "Does not block development") rather than as missing facts. The only `[NOTE FOR PM]` (§8.2) is a bare tag with no stated question.

### Findings
- **high** Privacy-notice and footer legal inputs are neither supplied nor asked for (§6 Legal; FR-3; §10) — FR-3 requires "the legal name" in the footer, and §6 requires the notice to name "kVA Instalaciones as the responsible party" and state "retention". Nowhere does the PRD give the registered legal name (razón social), the responsible party's domicile (an aviso de privacidad content requirement under the LFPDPPP), or a retention period. None of these is in §10. A "generic notice that meets these points" cannot be written without them. *Fix:* Add §10 Q6, owned by kVA, for the legal name, domicile, and retention period, and mark it launch-blocking. List domicile among the required notice elements in §6.
- **medium** The required email field duplicates what `mailto:` already provides (FR-11) — the email the Visitor sends already carries their address in the From header. Requiring them to type it again, plus a phone number that is required only by `[ASSUMPTION: both required]`, adds friction to a funnel whose target is a single Lead a month. SM-C1 warns against "adding fields," yet this friction is never weighed. *Fix:* Record the rationale (for example, to capture a work address when the Visitor sends from a personal device), or make email optional. Mark the phone requirement as a `[NOTE FOR PM]` decision, not an assumption.
- **low** Bare `[NOTE FOR PM]` (§8.2 WhatsApp bullet) — the tag has no question or decision attached. *Fix:* State the question explicitly, for example: "Should launch wait for the WhatsApp number, given the conversion research, or ship email-only?"

## Substance over theater — strong
There is no persona, vision, or NFR theater. Each UJ protagonist drives a distinct set of features. Laura (UJ-1) drives Service-CTA pre-fill and the no-mail-client edge case (FR-6, FR-12). Jorge (UJ-2) drives the filter, detail view, and Trust (FR-7, FR-8, FR-10). Ana (UJ-3) drives the copy-address path. The Vision ("*these people do serious electrical work, to standard*") is tied to kVA's actual credibility problem, the unfinished template, and the brief's statement that the "only differentiator … is 3+ years". It could not be swapped into another PRD. The NFRs carry real thresholds (1 MB / 8 kB budgets, LCP ≤ 2.5 s, 24×24 px targets, 4.5:1 contrast). Details such as the GDMTH tariff and CFE penalties show the domain knowledge is earned. No findings.

## Strategic coherence — adequate
The thesis is clear: credibility within seconds, then contact one step away. The features follow it: FR-2 and FR-6 keep contact one step away, and Trust, Projects, and FR-9 build credibility. The scope kind is a problem-solving MVP, and the scope logic matches. The counter-metrics SM-C1 and SM-C2 are real counterweights. SM-C2 directly protects NFR-1 against a design push.

The weak point is that the thesis can ship unvalidated. The brief says the angle "holds only if the page can show evidence". Under the PRD, launch can happen with every Project labelled "Ejemplo Ilustrativo" (FR-9). Trust can also be hidden entirely (FR-10), because nothing other than "3+ years" is confirmed. §10 Q2 only says real content is "Needed before Placeholder projects are replaced", which is circular.

### Findings
- **high** No minimum-proof launch bar for an evidence-dependent thesis (§1 Vision; FR-9; FR-10; §10 Q2) — a risk-averse buyer (UJ-2 Jorge "vets kVA") could reach a page whose only proof is placeholders and possibly an empty Trust section. Nothing in the PRD prevents this or records that the risk was accepted. *Fix:* Define a launch gate, for example "at least 2 real Projects and at least 2 confirmed Trust items, one of them a standard or registration." Otherwise, add a `[NOTE FOR PM]` recording that kVA accepts launching with placeholder-only proof.
- **medium** SM-1 cannot see one of the two mandated contact paths (SM-1; §3 Lead; UJ-3; FR-12) — a Lead is defined as an email that "carries the Lead subject tag". Ana (UJ-3) and every Copy-address fallback user write their own email with no tag, so by the glossary they are not Leads and SM-1 does not count them. The primary metric undercounts by design, and the fallback that FR-12 makes mandatory shows up as zero. *Fix:* Widen the Lead definition, or add a manual count of untagged first-contact emails that mention the site, and state the expected undercount in SM-1.
- **medium** UJ-1's search-acquisition path has no supporting requirement (UJ-1 "searches on her phone and lands on the Hero"; FR-16; addendum "Prerendering … optional") — on a single page, Service copy reaches crawlers only if the body is prerendered. The PRD leaves prerendering optional and defers per-Service pages. If it is left out, the brief's "a buyer searching … never finds kVA" problem stays unsolved. *Fix:* Either require Service names and descriptions in the served HTML (that is, make SSG mandatory), or reframe UJ-1 as referral or direct traffic and record search reach as a v2 bet.
- **low** SM-1's target wording is ambiguous ("1 or more per month within 3 months of launch") — it is unclear whether this means every month from month 3 onward or at least one month inside the first 3. *Fix:* Restate it, for example: "≥1 qualified Lead in each calendar month from month 3 after launch."

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
Most FRs have testable consequences that are good enough to write behaviour models and specs from. Examples include FR-7 (`aria-pressed`, a live count, and no network request), FR-11 (URL-encoding, `aria-describedby`, focus on the first invalid field), FR-16 (metadata present without JavaScript), and FR-17 (E.164 validation fails the build). The problems are one internal contradiction and a handful of adjectives where bounds are needed.

### Findings
- **high** FR-5 and FR-7 contradict each other on Project filter options — FR-5 says adding a fifth Service doc "produces five cards, five Project filter options". FR-7 says options are "'Todos' plus one per Service that has at least one Project". A new Service with no Projects therefore yields no new filter option, and with Projects the count is six including "Todos". Under test-first rules, the two consequences produce tests that cannot both pass. *Fix:* Change FR-5 to: "…and one more Project filter option once that Service has at least one Project (FR-7)."
- **medium** "Typical phone" and "or is reachable with one tap" are not bounds (FR-4, FR-2) — FR-4's above-the-fold check needs a reference viewport. In FR-2, "stays visible (or is reachable with one tap)" allows two different designs and cannot be tested as written. FR-1's "mobile breakpoint" is undefined, although design tokens can supply it. *Fix:* Name a reference viewport, for example 360×640 CSS px. Choose one option for the persistent CTA, or explicitly hand the choice to `bmad-ux`. Reference the breakpoint token.
- **medium** The Contact composer's form factor is undecided: "opens or scrolls to" (FR-2, FR-4, FR-6) — an inline section and an overlay panel need different state machines (focus trap, Esc, Back) under constitution II. FR-14 also has to preserve composer state while the notice is open. *Fix:* Decide inline section versus panel, or state that `bmad-ux` decides it and that the behaviour model follows that decision.
- **medium** FR-15's "every user-visible string" has an unclear scope and a test that cannot be decided mechanically — the list (Hero, Services, …, SEO) leaves out header nav labels, validation errors, "Todos", "Copiado", "Ejemplo Ilustrativo", the FR-12 message, and aria-labels. FR-9 and FR-12 quote some of these strings directly. "A test fails if a component contains literal Spanish copy" cannot be implemented reliably as stated. *Fix:* Put all UI chrome strings in scope through a `ui-strings` Content doc. Define the check concretely, for example: "no text nodes or `aria-label`/`alt`/`title` literals in templates except bindings."
- **medium** NFR-1's Core Web Vitals cannot be measured as written — "mid-range phone over 4G" is not a test profile. INP is a field metric, but there is no analytics (§7), and a low-traffic site will not have CrUX data. *Fix:* Define a lab profile, for example Lighthouse mobile default throttling. Use TBT ≤ 200 ms as the lab proxy for INP. Measure against the built static output so the check does not depend on the deferred production host.
- **medium** "Restrictive Content Security Policy" is an adjective (NFR-4) — the JSON-LD inline script (FR-16) and any web font (font choice is still open in §10 Q5) affect the policy directly. *Fix:* State a baseline, for example `default-src 'self'`, hashed inline JSON-LD, `frame-ancestors 'none'`, and self-hosted fonts. Note that a `<meta>` CSP is acceptable while hosting headers are deferred.
- **low** The Project detail view's content behaviour is underspecified (FR-8, FR-9) — the PRD does not say how multiple photos behave (gallery, carousel, or stacked), or whether the "Ejemplo Ilustrativo" label also shows inside the detail view. *Fix:* Add one consequence for each.

## Scope honesty — adequate
§7 Non-Goals does real work: no CMS, no backend or analytics, no English version (while keeping the architecture open to one), and no live chat. §8.2 is honest about deferrals and gives reasons. All nine inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags are indexed in §11. Open-items density is 9 assumptions, 5 Open Questions, and 1 `[NOTE FOR PM]`. That is acceptable for building, because every open item is explicitly non-blocking for development.

The problem is that development and launch are treated as the same thing. Q3, Q4, and Q5 each say they do not block development, but the PRD never says what blocks *launch*. Deployment is out of MVP scope, yet several requirements can only be met in production.

### Findings
- **high** "Launch" is undefined and unowned while NFRs and SMs depend on it (§8.2 "Hosting, DNS cutover, and deployment pipeline" out of scope; NFR-4 "HTTPS only"; SM-1 "within 3 months of launch"; SM-2 "on the production URL"; FR-16 canonical `https://kvainstalaciones.mx/`) — on a launch-stakes PRD for a live client domain, no requirement or owner makes the site go live. Epic creation will produce no stories for it. *Fix:* Add a short "Launch readiness" list that separates dev-blocking items from launch-blocking ones. The launch-blocking list should cover legal inputs, minimum proof, logo variants, hosting with HTTPS and CSP, and DNS cutover. Name an owner for deployment, even if that work sits outside the build epics.
- **low** Unacknowledged exposure and sourcing risks (FR-12, FR-16, FR-9) — publishing proyectos1@kvainstalaciones.mx in plain text and in JSON-LD invites spam to the inbox SM-1 depends on. Placeholder project photos need a licensed source, and the supplied logo (406×213, black background) is not a usable Open Graph image. *Fix:* Add one line for each: accept the spam risk or obfuscate, require licensed or own imagery for placeholders, and add an OG image asset to Q5.

## Downstream usability — adequate
This is the PRD's strongest structural feature. The glossary has 15 defined terms used consistently in bold, and the UJs have named protagonists with context. FR-1 to FR-17, NFR-1 to NFR-5, and SM-1 to SM-3 (plus SM-C1 and SM-C2) are unique and resolve. The addendum's "Constitution mapping" lists the behaviour-model IDs and schema names `bmad-architecture` needs. The remaining gaps sit exactly where the constitution's non-negotiable gates apply.

### Findings
- **medium** The behaviour-model list misses user actions (§6 Constitution; addendum "Constitution mapping") — the mobile menu open/close (FR-1) and the Hero secondary CTA (FR-4) are user actions with states, but neither is listed. Constitution II stops a story from entering **Act** without a behaviour model. The schema list also has no home for the UI chrome strings that FR-15 requires (see the FR-15 finding). *Fix:* Add a `mobile-menu` model, or state that `section-navigation` covers the menu and the Hero secondary CTA. Add a `ui-strings` schema, or say which existing schema (for example `site`) carries those strings.

## Shape fit — strong
This is a multi-stakeholder B2B marketing page that feeds UX, architecture, and stories. Its shape fits: three UJs with named protagonists are load-bearing, and FRs are grouped by page section. Technology is kept in the addendum, which suits a chain-top PRD. It is neither over- nor under-formalized; 17 FRs is proportionate for one page. No findings.

## Mechanical notes
- **ID order:** FR-17 sits in §4.6 between FR-13 and FR-14, so the document order is FR-13, FR-17, FR-14. The IDs are unique and resolve, but sequential readers may assume FR-17 is missing. Move it, or note that it was appended.
- **Assumptions Index roundtrip:** there are 9 inline tags and 9 index entries, and they match one-to-one. One entry is broader than its tag: the §11 FR-11 entry adds "phone is a 10-digit Mexican number", but the inline tag covers only "both required".
- **Glossary drift:** FR-3's "legal name" is used as if it differs from the brand **kVA Instalaciones**, but it is never defined (see the legal-inputs finding). "Trust section" (UJ-2) and "header contact CTA" (FR-2) are bolded or used like glossary terms but are not defined; this is minor. "Data services" is defined three times (§3, §4.3, addendum). Keep only the §3 definition.
- **Cross-references:** the brief and addendum links (`../../briefs/brief-agency-portfolio-2026-10-19/…`), `assets/media/icon.png`, the `angular.json` budgets (500 kB/1 MB, 4 kB/8 kB), and "(§10, Q3)" all resolve correctly.
- **Required sections:** all sections expected for this stakes level and product type are present.
